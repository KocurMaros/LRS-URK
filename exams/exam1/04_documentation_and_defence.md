# E1.4 — Documentation and defence (2 points)

The documentation is not a formality — it is where you show that you *chose* your solution
rather than stumbled into it. It lives in your repository (Markdown or PDF) and is submitted
together with the code.

Keep it short and dense. Three to six pages of substance beats twenty pages of padding, and
padding is easy to spot.

## Specification

### 1. Analysis of the approaches used — 1.0 point

For each significant decision you made, cover:

- **What you chose** and how it works — a short explanation of the algorithm or mechanism, in
  your own words. Enough that a classmate could follow it without reading your code.
- **Why you chose it** — what the alternatives were and what made this one better *for this
  problem*.
- **Pros and cons** — including the cons. Every choice has them; a page with no drawbacks
  listed reads as a page written without thinking.

At minimum, cover:

| Topic | Questions your text should answer |
|---|---|
| Map representation | Voxel grid / octree — which, what resolution, why that resolution? |
| Obstacle inflation | What radius, and how did you arrive at that number? How do you stop the planner routing through the shelving racks? |
| Planning algorithm | Which algorithm, which heuristic or sampling strategy, how long does it take, does it give an optimal path? |
| Path post-processing | What did you remove, how many points before and after, is the result still collision-free? |
| Position controller | What are the `hard` and `soft` tolerances, and why those values? How do you decide you have "arrived"? How do you avoid stopping at every intermediate path point? |
| Heading policy | How do you face the direction of travel between waypoints, and how do you unwrap the angle? |
| Geodetic conversion | Which method, what error does it have over your mission area? |
| Angle conventions | Where is ENU used, where NED, where compass heading — and where do you convert? |
| Figure-8 parameters | `A = 3 m` is fixed — what `ω` did you choose, what maximum speed does that give, and does it fit the geofence? |

If something does not work, **write that down too**, with what you think the cause is. An
honest known-limitations section scores; a solution presented as flawless that visibly fails at
the defence does not.

### 2. Overall solution diagram — 0.5 points

At least two diagrams, or one good one covering both:

- **Data processing path** — from the map file and the mission file, through planning and
  post-processing, to the setpoints being published. What transforms the data at each step.
- **ROS control diagram** — your nodes, the topics and services between them and MAVROS, and
  which callback groups / threads they run in. `rqt_graph` output is a starting point, but a
  hand-drawn diagram showing *your* structure is worth more than an autogenerated one.

A photo of a whiteboard sketch is acceptable if it is legible. Tools that work well:
[draw.io](https://app.diagrams.net/), [Mermaid](https://mermaid.js.org/) (renders directly in
Markdown on GitHub), or Inkscape.

### 3. Defence — 0.5 points

During the exercise in week 6 (or earlier, when a sub-exam is finished):

- **Run the mission** from a clean checkout of your repository.
- **Explain your code** — you will be asked about specific parts of it.
- **Make a small change on request** — for example: change the inflation radius, the figure-8
  amplitude, or a tolerance, and show the effect. This is quick if you parameterised things as
  the assignment asks, and painful if you hardcoded them.
- If you worked in a team, **every member defends individually** and must be able to explain
  the whole solution, not only their own part.

## Checklist before submitting

- [ ] The mission runs with the three simulation terminals brought up by hand, on a machine
      that is not yours.
- [ ] `README.md` explains dependencies, build steps and how to run each part of the mission.
- [ ] Mission files and parameter files are committed.
- [ ] Documentation covers every row of the table above.
- [ ] Both diagrams are present and readable.
- [ ] Rosbag or recording of the indoor mission and of the outdoor mission is committed or linked.
- [ ] You pushed **before the deadline** and messaged your teacher on Teams with the commit hash.
